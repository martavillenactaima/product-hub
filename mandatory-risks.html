What this feature does

⚙️
All clients

Base config — Global mandatory risk field

The buyer can mark risk declaration as mandatory at the account level. No employee can be assigned to a contract without declaring at least one risk.

🎯
Add-on

Activity-specific minimum risks

The buyer defines, per activity, which specific risks are minimum-mandatory. The platform validates collective coverage: the contractor's employees assigned to that activity must cover them between them all.

Validation scenarios

1

Employee assignment

When a contractor assigns an employee to a contract, the platform validates that the required risks for the activity are collectively covered by that contractor's assigned workforce.

2

Removal blocked

Removing an employee from an active contract is blocked if that employee is the sole holder of a minimum-mandatory risk for the activity — coverage must not break.

3

Reactivation after expiry edge case

If a contract expired and the sole risk-holder was removed during that period: on reactivation the system detects the gap, notifies the contractor, and blocks all their employees' access until coverage is restored.

Jobs to Be Done identified

Ranking #1 · Top priority

Job 1 — Buyer: enforce a baseline risk requirement across all assignments

When I need to ensure every contractor employee has declared risks, I want to configure risk declaration as mandatory at the account level, so I can eliminate the current gap where assignments are saved with no risks at all.

Functional: global rule — assignment cannot be saved with an empty risk field
Emotional: relief and confidence — no more anxiety over uncontrolled gaps in safety records
Social: be seen as a rigorous, audit-ready safety manager who doesn't leave compliance to chance
🟢 Direct evidence
«hoy por hoy se puede guardar el contrato sin indicar los riesgos, porque no son obligatorios.» — Aleksandra, Service team · Confirmed in Lankabe session
Ranking #2 · High priority

Job 2 — Buyer: enforce activity-specific risk coverage across the contractor's workforce

When a contractor is working on a specific activity (e.g. electrical work, elevated platforms), I want to define which risks are minimum-mandatory for that activity and have the platform validate collective coverage, so I can guarantee activity-specific risks are documented — not just the generic default.

Functional: per-activity risk rules + collective validation (≥1 employee covers each required risk)
Emotional: control — knowing the most dangerous activities have their specific risks covered
Social: demonstrate to auditors that risk management is granular and activity-specific, not one-size-fits-all
🟢 Direct evidence
«es súper recurrente que el contratista va al obligatorio y lo que no es obligatorio, no lo marca.» — Laura Méndez, CTAIMA PM · Lankabe session
Ranking #3 · Needs validation

Job 3 — Contractor: navigate risk requirements without being blocked unexpectedly

When assigning employees to a contract with mandatory risk rules, I want to understand clearly which risks are required and see real-time coverage feedback, so I can complete the assignment without surprise blocks and plan ahead which employees can cover the required risks.

Functional: visibility of required risks per activity before assigning + real-time coverage progress as employees are added
Emotional: avoid frustration of being blocked mid-process without understanding why — predictability reduces anxiety
Social: appear competent and organized to the buyer; avoid failing compliance checks repeatedly

Implied by the configuration affecting contractors directly. Supported indirectly by Virginia (Erki): «no sé decirle qué documentación le va a pedir». No direct contractor quote — needs contractor-side interviews.

Emotional Peaks & Valleys

🔴 Lowest point — Phase 5: the buyer has gone through the effort of requesting config from Service AND chasing the contractor, yet has zero enforcement. All that effort with no guarantee. This is where the most value can be created.
🔴 Second lowest — Phase 2: having to ask Service for something that should be self-configurable. Amplified for buyers who migrated from CTAIMACAE and remember having direct control. Feels like a step backwards.
🟡 Phase 4: manual contractor chasing via WhatsApp/email is invisible to Twind and creates a parallel coordination layer the product can never see or optimise.

Invisible Steps (outside the product)

These steps happen entirely outside Twind and represent the real competition — not other SaaS tools, but the workarounds buyers have built:

  • Phone calls and WhatsApp messages to contractor contacts
  • Internal tickets or email requests to the Service team
  • Manual Excel tracking of which contractors/employees have declared risks
  • Verbal agreements with contractors that are never recorded in the platform

⚠️ Critical Design Question — Rule Activation with Existing Assignments

Option Behaviour Trade-off
A — Retroactive All existing non-compliant assignments are immediately flagged; contractors notified; grace period defined; access blocked after deadline Full compliance enforced immediately — but high disruption risk if many existing assignments are affected
B — Forward-only Rules apply only to new assignments from activation date; existing assignments untouched Zero disruption — but the existing compliance gap persists indefinitely for already-assigned employees

⚠️ This decision must be made before designing the configuration UX. It affects the activation flow, notifications, access control integration, and the onboarding experience for buyers enabling the feature for the first time.

  • For the rule activation edge case: does the team have a preference for Option A or B, or is this still open?

Key Patterns

1. The enforcement gap is total and everything else flows from it. Fix enforcement, and several downstream pains are eliminated automatically.
2. The buyer has zero technical leverage over contractor behaviour inside the platform. All enforcement today is manual and happens outside Twind — phone calls, WhatsApp, Service requests. These workarounds are invisible to the product.
3. Contractors behave rationally — they do exactly what the platform requires, which today is nothing. This is a missing constraint problem, not a behaviour problem.
4. The pain is sharper for CTAIMACAE migrants. They experience Twind's gap as a regression, creating higher urgency and willingness to adopt.
5. Cap A and Cap B share the same root cause but diverge sharply in complexity. Cap B requires a new data model, coverage logic, and state management — with 2 open design questions that must be resolved before building.

Notable Variation

• Regression vs. missing feature: CTAIMACAE migrants feel this as a step backwards — more urgent and emotional. New buyers see it as a new capability — lower urgency, positive reception.
• Contract complexity varies by buyer: Simple single-activity vs. complex multi-activity — Cap B lands very differently. Config UX must handle both without overwhelming simpler buyers.
• Contractor visibility pain (row 5) appeared in only 1 of 3 sessions. May not be universal — needs validation before treating as a primary driver.

Devil's Advocate

Is the gap actually causing harm? We don't know how often assignments are saved with 0 risks in practice. A quick data pull on % of assignments with 0 risks would validate or deflate the urgency.
CTAIMACAE regression may be a vocal minority. Buyers who never used CTAIMACAE won't feel this pain the same way — total addressable pain may be smaller than it appears from these sessions.
Capability B adoption risk. If buyers find activity-level coverage complex to configure, adoption of the add-on may be low even after launch. The config UX needs to be simple or the feature goes unused.
Key Question

Before moving to Pain Mapping: what pain do you know exists that has not appeared in any of these sources? Particularly: are there CS escalations, support tickets, or sales objections about mandatory risks not represented in these 3 sessions?

• Accept the gap — no enforcement = no audit trail if an incident occurs

Success = configure once, enforce automatically, trust the result. No more manual chasing, no more uncertainty.
