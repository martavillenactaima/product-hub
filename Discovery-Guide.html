NPI Process — Simplified Guide

The full process, Steps 1–15: Discovery → Validation → NPI → Driving Adoption → Roadmap & Outcomes. For first iterations — learn the way of thinking, not perfection in every box.

1

Loops, not straight lines

Go back to any step when new information changes your thinking. That's the process working, not failing.

2

Order beats depth

Each step builds on the previous one. A light pass through all steps beats a perfect pass through two.

3

Evidence over opinion

At every step ask: do I know this from customers, or am I assuming it? Both are fine — but label them.

⭐ First time? The core path. If you only have time for the essentials, don't skip Step 3 (Transcripts), Step 4 (Pain Map), Step 5 (North Star), Step 6 (Product Brief) and Step 10 (Prototype). Steps 1–2 can be light; Steps 7–9 enrich your ideas; Steps 11–12 matter when heading to launch; Steps 13–14 kick in once live; Step 15 works at any time — it's a roadmap audit, not a launch step.
📁 Deliverables & naming convention: each step produces one document — in .md or in Confluence, your choice per step. Every title follows the same pattern: NN Step Name — Feature.

Example — feature "Marketplace Tiering":
· Confluence page: 03 Transcript Analysis — Marketplace Tiering
· Markdown file: 03-transcript-analysis-marketplace-tiering.md (title in kebab-case)

Keep everything for one feature in the same folder / Confluence parent page — later steps read earlier deliverables, and the AI companion finds them by this naming pattern.

Phase 1 — Discovery

Understand users, workflows and pain before building anything.

1

JTBD Review

Deliverable: 01 JTBD Review — [Feature]
flexible
✓
▼

The idea

A Job to be Done is the progress a user is trying to make in a specific circumstance — not a feature request. The job exists regardless of your product. Acid test: "Would this job still exist if our product disappeared?"

When [situation], I want to [motivation], so I can [expected outcome].
  • Functional — what they need to accomplish
  • Emotional — how they want to feel (confidence, relief, control)
  • Social — how they want to be seen (competent, thorough)

The last two often drive adoption more than the first. Watch out for features disguised as jobs ("I want a dashboard") and several jobs mashed into one vague statement.

Simplified version: write 2–3 JTBD, tag each one evidence or assumption, and rank them. That's enough.
Key question: which JTBD is grounded in real customer evidence — and which is a hypothesis I'm carrying in?
2

Workflow Map

Deliverable: 02 Workflow Map — [Feature]
flexible
✓
▼

The idea

You cannot reimagine what you don't understand. Map how the user gets the job done today — including everything outside your product. The invisible steps (the email thread, the side spreadsheet, the Slack ping) are where your real competition lives: not another tool, but the workaround.

Map across five dimensions: Phase · Doing · Thinking & Feeling · Touchpoints · Opportunities. Then find the emotional peaks and valleys — the lowest point is usually where the biggest opportunity lives.

Simplified version: pick ONE user and ONE job. Narrate the journey in plain language — the AI structures the table. Just answer the two key questions.
Key questions: which steps of this journey do I never see? Where are the highest and lowest points?
3

Transcript Analysis

Deliverable: 03 Transcript Analysis — [Feature]
★ core
✓
▼

The idea

Let real customer conversations tell you where the pain is. The AI surfaces 5–8 recurring themes with quotes and frequency — but you define the taxonomy. Whoever owns the taxonomy owns the framing of the problem space.

  • Strategies > Challenges. The workarounds users already built reveal more than their complaints.
  • Underlying pains — implicit, never stated directly. The insights competitors miss.
  • Emotional signals predict willingness-to-pay. Listen for "every single time…", "I can't believe we still…"

Finish with a devil's advocate pass: alternative explanations? Preferences disguised as problems? What would a skeptic push back on?

Simplified version: even 3–5 transcripts work. Themes + quotes + your taxonomy is the minimum; the full tagged table can come in a second pass.
Key question: what pain do I know exists that didn't show up here?
4

Pain Mapping

Deliverable: 04 Pain Mapping — [Feature]
★ core
✓
▼

The idea

Plot every pain on a 2×2: Frequency (daily / weekly / occasionally) × Friction (workaround / manual / blocked). Top-right — high friction, high frequency — is where the value is.

Then pick one anchor pain. Not the only pain you'll ever solve — the one that anchors your product brief, north star and narrative. Scattered focus produces scattered products.

Intuition check: does the ranking match what you hear in sales calls and CS escalations? If not, investigate. If you change the anchor later, change it explicitly — never let it drift silently.

Key question: does this ranking match my intuition? What feels more important than where it landed?
5

User Outcome & North Star

Deliverable: 05 User Outcome & North Star — [Feature]
★ core
✓
▼

The idea

Turn the anchor pain into two statements everything downstream traces back to.

[User segment] needs [user outcome] so that [value/impact].

Checks: ① the segment also says who you're NOT targeting ② the outcome is a result, not a feature in disguise ③ the impact is testable.

We hypothesize that if [segment] can [achieve outcome] through [core behaviour], as measured by [North Star Metric], we will drive [business outcome].

Connect to one of the five value drivers — GRR · NRR · NCA · AOV · WR — with a number. "Improve retention" is not an outcome; "improve GRR from 85% to 90% in 6 months" is. Vague outcomes can't fail — so they can't teach you anything.

Key question: is this outcome valuable enough that the user will actually change their behaviour?
🧪

Phase 2 — Validation

Build the case, test assumptions — earn the right to build.

6

Product Brief

Deliverable: 06 Product Brief — [Feature]
★ core
✓
▼

The idea

Crystallize Phase 1 into a sharp business case — not a 50-page PRD. Six sections, each with a specificity bar:

SectionThe bar to clear
Problem Statement[role] does [X] in [context], takes [time] or fails [rate]
Target Userrole + company type + the moment the problem occurs
User Outcome[user] does [X] in [timeframe] with [quality measure]
Product Outcome[X%] of [segment] doing [behaviour] by [date]
Business Outcomevalue driver + a monetary figure (rough is fine)
Competitive Landscape[X%] use [alt] — none solve [gap] because [structural reason]

The brief is a hypothesis engine. Close by naming your 2 weakest assumptions + a quick validation test for each — days, not weeks.

Key question: does my business outcome have a number attached? If not, what's my best estimate?
7

Workflow Re-imagination

Deliverable: 07 Workflow Re-imagination — [Feature]
flexible
✓
▼

The idea

Take the Step 2 map and reimagine it AI-first. Classify each stage:

  • Automated — AI end-to-end (only where errors are cheap)
  • Augmented — AI assists, human in the loop (where judgment matters)
  • Eliminated — no longer needed. The highest-value outcome.
  • Unchanged — human judgment non-negotiable

Weigh per stage: regulation, data availability, user trust, feasibility today. The ambition test: if it just makes steps 20% faster, you're thinking incrementally — what if a junior with AI could do what a senior does today? The biggest current-vs-reimagined gap = your product opportunity.

Key question: does this eliminate steps entirely, or change who does the work?
8

Crazy 8s + AI Ideation

Deliverable: 08 Crazy 8s & AI Ideation — [Feature]
flexible
✓
▼

The idea

8 ideas in 8 minutes, one line each, no filtering. At least 2 must be impossible without AI. The discipline is speed, not quality — filtering too early kills the best ideas, because the best ideas sound unreasonable at first. Judgment comes in Step 9.

Then let the AI find what's missing (ideas that eliminate a step, AI-first paradigms, adjacent-industry solutions, ideas that change who does the work) and stretch you with: MacGyver Mindset (strip domain labels, reassemble the parts), Synectics (force an analogy from another domain), Opposite Thinking (design the most frustrating version, flip each into a principle).

Key question: which direction feels worth pursuing? Does anything combine with one of my original 8?
9

Opportunity Solution Tree

Deliverable: 09 Opportunity Solution Tree — [Feature]
flexible
✓
▼

The idea

One tree: business outcome at the trunk → 3–5 opportunities as branches (framed as "users struggle with…", grounded in Steps 3–4) → sub-opportunities → your Crazy 8s ideas as leaves (hypotheses, not commitments).

Two diagnostics fall out automatically:

  • Ideas mapping to no opportunity = solutions looking for a problem → park them.
  • Opportunities with no ideas = your next ideation focus — more valuable than your current best ideas.
Simplified version: a simple indented list is fine — the structure matters, not the drawing. It's a living document.
Key question: any opportunities with nothing underneath? That's my discovery gap.
10

Prototype

Deliverable: 10 Prototype Scope — [Feature]
★ core
✓
▼

The idea

Prototype the idea you're most uncertain about — not the one you like best. A prototype exists to reduce risk; the riskiest idea teaches you the most. Prototyping what you're already confident about is seeking validation, not learning.

Answer before building:

  • What is the single most important interaction to test?
  • What must a user see or do in 2 minutes to judge the concept?
  • What's the riskiest assumption this tests? ← can't answer? You're building a demo, not a prototype.

Build with an AI tool (a Claude Artifact is enough). Match fidelity to the assumption: low-fi tests viability, hi-fi tests usability. Before engineering: what numbers must be right? What data does it depend on? What happens when data is missing?

Key question: what's the riskiest assumption this prototype should test?
🚀

Phase 3 — New Product Introduction

Arm product marketing, sales, CS and support.

11

Value Proposition Brief

Deliverable: 11 Value Proposition Brief — [Feature]
at launch
✓
▼

The idea

Translate discovery into what PMM and Sales need: target segment & personas · the problem and today's workarounds · what success looks like · the three JTBD dimensions · value drivers in money · competitive positioning.

Two rules make or break it:

  • Success in the customer's words, not yours. Pull exact phrases from transcripts. If your positioning uses words no customer ever said, it describes what you want the product to be.
  • Show the math. "Saves time" is not a value driver. "Saves 4 h/analyst/week × €95/h ≈ €19,760/analyst/year" is. A rough number that can be challenged beats a vague claim that can't be questioned.

Position on structural gaps ("X can't do this because their architecture requires…") — execution gaps ("we're faster") just invite a response.

Key question: are the € amounts specific enough to be challenged?
12

Launch FAQ

Deliverable: 12 Launch FAQ — [Feature]
at launch
✓
▼

The idea

One source of truth for every GTM team. Ten sections: overview & pitch · target market and ICP (who NOT to sell to matters as much) · pricing · positioning per competitor · implementation · client success signals · support playbook · AI FAQs · objection handling · GTM readiness checklist.

The two sections deserving most care:

  • AI FAQs (non-negotiable with AI features): accuracy & evals, hallucination safeguards, data handling, explainability, liability. Sales and CS will get these questions before you expect them.
  • Objections: top 5 from real win/loss — acknowledge → reframe with evidence → bridge to proof point.

In the readiness checklist, separate "must be true before any customer sees this" from "should be true before scaling".

Simplified version: start with overview, ICP and objections; use [placeholder] everywhere else and fill in as launch approaches.
Key question: could my team actually deliver the AI FAQ answers today?

Phase 4 — Driving Adoption

Adoption is now a core PM responsibility — not a handoff to Sales and CS.

13

Metrics & Adoption Dashboard

Deliverable: 13 Metrics & Adoption Dashboard — [Feature]
once live
✓
▼

The idea

Derive the outcome chain — three linked layers everything else hangs on:

  • User outcome — what behaviour change are you driving?
  • Product outcome — what does adoption look like at scale? (your North Star metric)
  • Business outcome — which value driver moves, and by how much in €?

Then build the dashboard: health status · outcome chain metrics · leading indicators (predict the North Star before lagging data shows it — each one defines the action it triggers if it drops) · lagging indicators (business outcomes in € — they confirm, but arrive too late to act on) · qualitative signals · action items.

Vanity metric rule: any metric that can't connect to the outcome chain is a vanity metric ("active users" without defining active, usage counts without outcome correlation). Flag them explicitly.

AI features? Add AI health: acceptance rate, edit distance, regeneration rate, thumbs-down by query type, failure mode alerts. Low acceptance isn't just a quality issue — it's an adoption risk.

Simplified version: start with just the outcome chain + 2–3 leading indicators with triggers. Where you can't measure, flag an instrumentation gap — don't fill in generic metrics.
Key question: can every metric on this dashboard connect to the outcome chain?
14

Activation Levers

Deliverable: 14 Activation Levers — [Feature]
once live
✓
▼

The idea

An adoption plan across three lever types, each owned by a different part of the pod:

Lever typeOwnerExamples
Digital adoption motionsProduct MarketingEmail nurture by segment & activation status, in-app onboarding, weekly tips
High-touch engagementCustomer Success1:1 consulting, Lunch & Learns, advisory groups — stratify accounts by LTV × activity (high LTV + low activity = top priority)
Product-led interventionsDesign & EngineeringFeature parity blockers, UX simplification of high drop-off flows, research with power AND churned users

The rule that makes it real — triggers. For each lever, define the dashboard signal that fires it: "if weekly active usage drops below X for a high-LTV account, CS schedules a check-in within 48 h." An activation plan without triggers is a wish list.

One-size-fits-all nurture signals that you don't understand your users — different segments need different messages at different times.

Key questions: which lever surprised you? Which segment is most at risk right now — where would you act first?
🎯

Phase 5 — Roadmap & Outcomes

Look at your roadmap honestly and decide what bet is actually worth making.

15

Evidence Audit & The Bigger Bet

Deliverable: 15 Evidence Audit & The Bigger Bet — [Feature]
anytime
✓
▼

The idea

The capstone. Audit every roadmap item across three dimensions:

  • Discovery — grounded in a high-friction, high-frequency pain from real evidence, or a stated wish / internal assumption?
  • Validation — has the riskiest assumption been tested? Could it be prototyped in a week before committing engineering?
  • Adoption — user/product/business outcome metrics defined, in €, with telemetry in place? (AI feature → eval plan?)

Label each item: Grounded · Plausible but untested · On the roadmap for other reasons (stakeholder request, competitive parity, legacy commitment — not inherently wrong, but it needs honest labelling).

The three bets

  • The Rescoped Bet — take the strongest-evidence item. Does the scope match the size of the pain, or are you solving a slice when evidence points to something bigger?
  • The Invested Bet — take the weakest-evidence item. Instead of building or cutting: what investment (discovery, prototyping, telemetry, EAP) would let you make a real decision next quarter? Specific: what, who, when.
  • The Capability Bet — what pattern do the gaps reveal? Mostly discovery gaps = under-investing in understanding users. Mostly validation gaps = shipping too fast. Mostly adoption gaps = under-investing in instrumentation. Name the capability — and one concrete action in the next 30 days.
Key question: which roadmap item did I walk in most confident about — and feel least confident about now?
